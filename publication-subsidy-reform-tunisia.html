Subsidy Reform in Tunisia: Who Benefits from the Current System? Incidence Evidence from the 2021 Household Budget Survey

Mohamed Baraa Lafi Paris School of Economics (PSE)
Share: Link copied! Citation copied!
Download Paper Download Data & Code

Abstract

This paper studies who actually benefits from Tunisia's consumer subsidy system for staple food and energy products. Using data at the household level from the 2021 national household budget survey, together with the official subsidy rates and total fiscal costs published in the 2025 Citizen Budget, I estimate how much of each subsidy reaches households directly, how that amount is distributed across the income distribution, and how much never reaches a measured household at all.

The results show wide variation across products. Some staples, particularly semolina and the larger loaf known as gros pain, are captured disproportionately by poor households and by the bottom sixty percent of the population. Others, especially gasoline and diesel, are captured mostly by richer households or never reach households at all, most likely absorbed by the transport and commercial sectors. Splitting bread into its two official categories, gros pain and baguette, reveals that they have very different distributional profiles even though official statistics usually report them together.

Combining these estimates, I calculate that limiting these subsidies to the bottom sixty percent of the population, while keeping diesel subsidised for everyone to avoid a shock to transport costs, would save the state close to four point seven billion dinars a year. That amount is more than seventy percent of current spending on these products and close to half of the government's projected budget deficit for 2025.

4. Potential fiscal savings from a targeted reform

For each product except diesel, the amount the state would save each year by limiting that subsidy to the bottom 60% of the population: the share currently going to the top 40% plus the share never reaching a household, multiplied by the official fiscal cost. Diesel is deliberately excluded and kept subsidised for everyone, since its price feeds directly into freight, public transport, and farming costs, and removing it even for richer households risks an economy wide price shock that would end up hurting the same poor households the reform is meant to protect.

Potential annual savings by product

Million Dinars · ranked from largest to smallest · diesel shown in grey, kept subsidised for everyone

Total potential savings: 4,689 Million Dinars, equivalent to 70.6% of the 6,646 Million Dinar combined fiscal cost of these eleven products, and 46.2% of the projected 2025 budget deficit of 10,150 Million Dinars. This is an upper bound assuming perfect targeting with no administrative cost, leakage, or behavioural response.

Data and methodology

  1. Institut National de la Statistique (INS), Tunisia. Enquête Nationale sur le Budget, la Consommation et le Niveau de Vie des Ménages (EBCNV) 2021. Household level microdata on expenditure, quantities, and official poverty status.
  2. Ministère des Finances, Tunisie. Budget Citoyen 2025. Official subsidy rates per unit and total fiscal cost by product.
  3. Household quantities are annualised from the survey's 7 day expenditure diary, after excluding supplementary recall entries that would otherwise double count the same purchases.
  4. Bread is reported as two separate official categories, gros pain and baguette, each matched to its own official weight, retail price, subsidy rate, and share of the basic goods budget, rather than a single blended rate.
