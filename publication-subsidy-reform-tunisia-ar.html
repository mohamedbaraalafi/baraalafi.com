Who Benefits from Food and Energy Subsidies in Tunisia? Incidence and Distributional Effects of Consumer Subsidies, Evidence from the 2021 Household Budget Survey

Mohamed Baraa Lafi Paris School of Economics (PSE)
Download Paper Download Data & Code

Abstract

This paper examines the distributional incidence of Tunisia's consumer subsidy system using household-level microdata from the 2021 National Survey on Household Budget, Consumption and Living Standards (EBCNV), combined with official fiscal cost figures from the Ministry of Finance's 2025 Citizen Budget. We reconstruct annual household expenditure on ten subsidized food and energy products, rank households by per-capita consumption, and compute population-weighted incidence shares by decile. The subsidy system as a whole is mildly regressive: the poorest decile captures 7.5% of total subsidy value against 13.8% for the richest, a gap a household-level bootstrap confirms is not sampling noise. Food staples are close to distributionally neutral or mildly pro-poor; transport fuel subsidies are strongly regressive.

Comparing household-survey expenditure against official fiscal totals reveals a second finding: for every product we can benchmark, the amount visible in household purchases is a fraction of the true budgetary cost — from 90% (essence) down to just 4% (gasoil) — the remainder consumed through commercial and institutional channels invisible to a household survey by construction. Electricity and gas is the single largest component in fiscal terms (3.91 billion DT of the roughly 10.7 billion DT covered here) with middling targeting. As a headline estimate: if subsidies were reformed so the richest 40% no longer received them, leaving the bottom 60% — including all poor households — covered as before, the state would save an estimated 4.71 billion DT annually, 44% of total fiscal cost.

Our product-level findings are also broadly consistent with the World Bank's own decile-based estimates for Tunisian energy subsidies (Tunisia Economic Monitor, Spring 2023), an external check obtained from an independent data source and method.
